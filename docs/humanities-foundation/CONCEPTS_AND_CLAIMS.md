# Seven questions a reconstruction can raise

One pixel can look convincing, differ from the photograph at its camera, and have a traceable source. A reader can inspect each of those claims separately. In the examples below, **R** supplied the reconstruction, **A** is the permitted source library, **Q** is the selection actually checked, and **H** supplies independent target observations. [Model lineage and source roles](MODEL_AND_DATA_BASIS.md)

| Question | What the available evidence can establish | Counterexample and remaining question |
|---|---|---|
| **1. How does the scene look across viewpoints?** | The six bell views let a reader inspect continuity of the cage bars, their apparent depth and what becomes occluded as a camera moves. | The project designed the bell and its cameras. A coherent sequence is possible for an invented object. How convincing or navigable it feels to a particular audience requires audience evidence. [Bell views and camera match](../releases/v0.5.0/bell-comparison/README.md) |
| **2. Does its colour agree with an independent observation?** | At a registered H camera, compare the predicted pixel with the independently obtained target in the same colour space. The reported error event is a mean absolute difference across three display-sRGB channels greater than 0.05 on a 0–1 scale. | A similar-coloured background can give a small difference at a pixel whose depicted surface has the wrong depth. Colour alone leaves surface identity, metric shape and unobserved viewpoints open. [RGB comparison](../releases/v0.5.0/bell-comparison/README.md) · [Independent geometry check](../research/many-source-v1/geometric-visibility.csv) |
| **3. Did Q contain a comparable source?** | The multi-source checker names a queried source where the projected position is in frame, the photograph and self-render are available, both predicted depths are positive and both opacities are at least 0.01. Stronger depth support requires both opacities to reach 0.5 and relative depth disagreement to stay within 0.10. | A source pair can qualify for comparison while disagreeing in depth or colour. In the gallery, the model found eight apparently comparable target-surface probe positions for which the independent mesh test found no visible source in the full selection. True visibility on a scene without such geometry remains open. [Feature meanings](../research/many-source-v1/feature-schema.json) · [Mesh counts](../research/many-source-v1/geometric-visibility.csv) |
| **4. Did the permitted library A contain a source?** | A separately recorded scan through the entire accessible library can identify a specific witness omitted by Q, or report that no permitted pair met the comparability rule. | The gallery's four-source check missed witnesses that later queries found. Missing source files, invalid target geometry and an exhausted library are different outcomes. Even a completed audit leaves material outside A unknown. [Query and audit rules](../research/many-source-v1/protocol.md) · [Audit counts](../research/many-source-v1/comparability.csv) |
| **5. What material made the reconstruction?** | Source identities, camera records, triangulation inputs and optimizer access show which R images contributed to initialization and fitting. The study checked the training names, images and cameras against each dispatch. | A hash can match every byte of a photograph taken after someone arranged a scene. The record can establish use of that photograph while the staging and events before capture remain open. An imported reconstruction with unknown training sources lacks this evidence. [Source audit](../research/many-source-v1/data/source-data-audit.json) · [Fit-input audit](../research/many-source-v1/input-alignment.json) |
| **6. Can someone inspect the process and its choices?** | Registered camera sets, query orders, grids, score cutoffs, code and opening records let another reader identify what was selected, repeat a calculation and see failures. | A reproducible centre crop still excludes its margins. Several photographs can repeat the same viewpoint or omission. A traceable selection leaves the adequacy of its coverage, and the reason for the original framing, open to inquiry. [Access order](../research/many-source-v1/access-order.json) · [Terminal outcomes and resource limits](../research/many-source-v1/resources-and-failures.md) |
| **7. Is a historical, cultural, semantic or social interpretation warranted?** | The capture and processing record identifies material on which an interpretation could draw and where other sources are needed. A claim about an object's date, use or significance needs contextual records, testimony or relevant scholarship. | The bell's designer and construction are documented. Even perfect reproduction of every designed view would supply no account of how a historical community used such a bell. The technical studies collected no audience testimony. [Authored bell provenance](../releases/v0.5.0/upstream/README.md) |

## A low score, a small error and an accepted pixel

On the authored bell, both routes used one four-source reconstruction and the same six cameras. On their common 256 × 144 field of view, independent references found **35,416 errors among 221,184 pixels** for the native landscape prediction and **34,989 among 221,184** for the legacy prediction. Among the positions scoreable by every model on both routes, MS-ft selected the lowest-ranked fifth in each view. That yielded **0 errors in 43,507 selected native pixels** and **1 in 43,507 selected legacy pixels**. The whole-image counts include the unselected positions. The legacy canvas also carried 56 padding rows above and 56 below the picture. Those rows have a known reason for their unknown state. [Full and common populations](../releases/v0.5.0/bell-comparison/populations.csv) · [Matched selections](../releases/v0.5.0/bell-comparison/coverage.csv)

The original single-source L1-full study reported 62.71% low coverage and 11.03% error within that group, averaging the four held-out families equally. Across their 24 targets, 419,324 of 2,043,247 confirmed errors received a low decision. That last fraction asks what share of *all errors* the low group contained. [Original held-out denominators](../releases/v0.1.1-rc.3/MODEL_CARD.md)

In the prospective T1 study, a finite MS-L1 score below the released cutoff of 0.06428983435034752 qualified as low. On physical DTU object `set103` with four sources, **199,114 / 393,216** reference-valid pixels received a low decision across six views. This is low **coverage**. Among those 199,114, **66,958 / 199,114** were confirmed RGB errors. Across the entire crop **164,123 / 393,216** were errors, and the scorer marked **38,725 / 393,216** unknown. The four-model matrix delivered 240 of 288 scheduled slots. Two of six four-source reconstructions stopped at the frozen checkpoint probe. Their reference pixels stay in the planned coverage denominator and their error rates are undefined. [T1 object counts and missing cases](../results/t1/README.md) · [Original score cutoffs](../releases/v0.5.0/BASELINE_MANIFEST.json)

C6 asked a separate operating question on six physical objects per layout. Could a cutoff give mean accepted-pixel error at most **5%**, mean coverage at least **20%**, and at least one accepted pixel on every object? At coverage of at least 20%, the lowest measured object-mean errors were **60.69%** for two sources and **15.15%** for four. Both profiles therefore report a null cutoff. The 0.05 RGB discrepancy decides whether one pixel is an error. The 5% target concerns the frequency of those errors among accepted pixels. [C6 curves, denominators and profiles](../results/batch-two/calibration/README.md)

## A source can be found while the picture stays the same

The preregistered gallery example is camera `h00` of `b05-perforated-gallery` reconstructed from 128 images. In its fixed prediction, **4,579 / 95,256** pixels exceeded the independent colour threshold. Among the other **90,677**, the coverage-first queries missed an available witness at **180, 120, 109, 12 and 0** positions for budgets **4, 8, 16, 32 and 128** respectively. The fixed four-source order missed only **74** on this particular view. Even with all 128 sources, **220 / 95,256** positions had no comparable witness anywhere in A under the model's rule. Each budget here used the same prediction. [View and prediction digest](../research/many-source-v1/views.csv) · [Per-budget counts](../research/many-source-v1/comparability.csv)

On external confirmation, the new head and the simple residual rule each selected 190,510 pixels per scene at the same 16-source budget. The new head selected **51,334** errors against the rule's **45,524** in `living_room`, and **44,943** against **46,014** in `bridge`. The registered criterion required fewer errors in both scenes and the result was `completed_negative`. Finding more available evidence and ranking independent colour errors are separately measurable outcomes. The reported difference concerns those cameras and two captured scenes.
