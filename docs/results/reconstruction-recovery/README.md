The selected reconstruction recovered the painted container on set1 and the roofs, windows and archway of the architectural miniature on set6. In the four-source set6 regression, mean target RGB error fell from 0.21362 to 0.08801. The released MS-L1 rule called 118,537 of 393,216 pixels low risk. These results cover one development object and one regression object. ## Where the old reconstructions lost support

At the identical 4,096-point start, the centres of 151 Gaussians projected into at least one of the two DTU source cameras. With four sources the count was 180. On the original synthetic rig the corresponding count was 2,441 in both conditions. Finite splat footprints also contribute when their centres lie outside the image. ## Synthetic control for the first recipe

On the authored bell cage, four-source self-render MAE fell from 0.00916 to 0.00259. The old target MAE was 0.01755. The new model reached 0.03435 after 5,000 updates, behind the nearest-axis source copy at 0.02114. Each of the six targets worsened against the old reconstruction. On the new renders 66,744 of 393,216 pixels exceeded RGB MAE 0.05 (16.97%), compared with 8.65% previously. The checkpoint at update 1,500 was saved immediately after the scheduled opacity reset. Its independent target MAE was 0.41106, falling to 0.05230 at update 3,000.

The new source views reproduce the crossbar, bell edges and legs sharply. New target views show a misplaced floor–wall boundary and smeared shadows. On v06 a large false foreground surface obscures the lower bells. Its target MAE rose from 0.02024 to 0.07285. The largest native projected radius in the last four source updates reached 2,367 pixels on a 256-pixel image. The fit began with 66 Gaussians, peaked at 18,202, and recorded zero capacity-blocked growth. The source–target gap and projected sizes support view-specific fitting as an explanation. Attribution of individual artefacts to particular Gaussians remains open. The Mac quality check used all five checkpoints from the same Windows optimization and all ten corresponding source/target renders per checkpoint.

## Why a second recipe was admitted

On the first set1 recipe, the can, red lettering and hand motif appeared in all six new views. M2 still smeared the base and side, and the lid extended above its true rim on several views. M4 reproduced the front and sides more clearly. At 3,000 updates, target MAE fell from 0.26028 to 0.14632 in M2 and from 0.20371 to 0.09514 in M4. Each of the twelve target images beat its old reconstruction and both source-only RGB references. Source MAE continued to decrease between 3,000 and 5,000 updates as the target means rose to 0.14968 and 0.09634.

A separate source-only checkpoint calculation projected the three-sigma ellipses. At step 3,000, zero image-intersecting ellipses had a major half-axis above 256 pixels on set1. At step 5,000 the count was two or three per M2 source and three or four per M4 source. One M4 Gaussian had a 1.674 m longest axis, opacity 0.0147 and a centre 0.615–0.654 m deep across the source cameras. On the authored control a longest axis reached 4.691 m against a 0.720 m camera baseline. This calculation measures size and overlap. It cannot identify which Gaussian caused a target pixel error. The cap blocked 15,179 of 37,845 growth nominations on set1 M2 and 95,002 of 117,246 on M4. These totals count nominations over multiple updates.

The second and last candidate changed one scalar, the terminal log-scale learning rate from 0.005 to 0.0005. The exponential schedule changes the rates throughout optimization, including the period of splitting and cloning. The source geometry, seed, cap, renderer and loss match the first candidate. A fresh native 20,000-point capacity check passed with this recipe before its first scientific fit. The scale hypothesis has one seed per condition. The registered choice uses six development targets, with equal M2 and M4 weights, across all eligible checkpoints of completed pairs.
