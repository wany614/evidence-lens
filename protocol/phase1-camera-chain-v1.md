## From the source PNG to the learned scene

The generator reads an RGB8 PNG, checks its dimensions against its source camera and requires centred, square-pixel intrinsics. It passes the original PNG array and its actual horizontal focal length in pixels into SHARP. The baseline source width is 512 and its focal length is 597⅓ px. The R1 source width is 256 and its focal length is 298⅔ px. Both give a focal-length-to-width ratio of 7/6. R1 transforms integer-centred source positions as `u′ = (u + 0.5)/2 − 0.5`. The six target cameras retain their baseline sizes, poses and intrinsics. Q1 takes the selected B0 PLY, rounds its three stored colour coefficients through binary16 and returns them to binary32. Its derivation records zero neural forward calls. The original generation receipts record the checkpoint digest, input size, focal length, declared network size and timed forward. The internal tensors, individual disparities and intermediate Gaussian coordinates of those historical forwards were not serialized.

SHARP converts the input bytes to floating RGB, divides by 255 and resizes both axes to 1536 with bilinear interpolation and endpoint alignment. It supplies the original focal length divided by the original width to its predictor. The initial metric-depth estimate divides that factor by estimated disparity. Learned updates also affect the final depth. The Gaussian composer emits `(Z·x_ndc, Z·y_ndc, Z)`. Its initializer samples the centre of each cell, with y increasing downwards. The executable unprojection maps the top-left boundary to `(−1,−1)`. An adjacent upstream comment labels its y coordinate differently. [SHARP preprocessing](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-sharp/blob/aed6527499ef91cba3b54c18d49a870f25947190/src/sharp/cli/predict.py#L159-L205) · [Depth estimate](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-sharp/blob/aed6527499ef91cba3b54c18d49a870f25947190/src/sharp/models/predictor.py#L126-L143) · [Cell coordinates](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-sharp/blob/aed6527499ef91cba3b54c18d49a870f25947190/src/sharp/models/initializer.py#L256-L271) · [Gaussian means](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-sharp/blob/aed6527499ef91cba3b54c18d49a870f25947190/src/sharp/models/composer.py#L188-L209)

The official unprojection uses the original boundary-centred principal point `(W/2,H/2)`, scales the horizontal and vertical intrinsic rows by `1536/W` and `1536/H`, and uses identity source extrinsics. Its inverse transform preserves the Gaussian's Z coordinate. The RGB interpolator and the continuous camera grid have different edge conventions. At internal integer index j, RGB samples original index `j(W−1)/1535`. A camera ray through that internal pixel centre corresponds to original index `(j+0.5)W/1536−0.5`. The maximum endpoint difference is one third of a source pixel at W=512 and five twelfths at W=256. These values follow from the two pinned formulas. They are not measurements of a learned depth-scale error. [Projection matrix and Gaussian transform](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-sharp/blob/aed6527499ef91cba3b54c18d49a870f25947190/src/sharp/utils/gaussians.py#L69-L121)

## From metric Gaussians to the target raster

The official PLY writer stores camera-frame Gaussian means, log-scales, opacity logits and rotation quaternions. It encodes linear colour into sRGB degree-zero spherical-harmonic coefficients, includes the original boundary-based intrinsics and width/height, and writes an identity source pose. The official loader restores linear RGB, opacity and scales. The renderer’s relative transform is `inverse(target camera-to-world) × source camera-to-world`. Public camera matrices use +x right, +y down and +z forward. Pixel (0,0) is the centre of the first pixel. The renderer adds 0.5 to both public principal coordinates before invoking gsplat 1.5.3, whose CUDA kernel samples at `(column+0.5,row+0.5)`. The two conventions describe the same ray. [PLY loader and writer](https://github.com/apple-aiml-research/ml-sharp/blob/aed6527499ef91cba3b54c18d49a870f25947190/src/sharp/utils/gaussians.py#L210-L436) · [Raster sample location](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/gsplat/blob/937e29912570c372bed6747a5c9bf85fed877bae/gsplat/cuda/csrc/RasterizeToPixels3DGSFwd.cu#L60-L65)

The projection kernel computes each Gaussian’s axial target-camera Z in metres. For weights `wᵢ = αᵢ × remaining transmittance`, the output channels give `A = Σwᵢ`, `D = Σwᵢ Zᵢ` and `ED = D/max(A,10⁻¹⁰)`. ED can fall between surfaces on a ray. Linear RGB accumulates against black. The adapter converts the final colour to display sRGB once, retains floating RGB and accumulated opacity, and makes depth unavailable at zero or invalid opacity, nonpositive Z or an unverified depth gate. Its PNG preview is the rounded display array. [Axial depth](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/gsplat/blob/937e29912570c372bed6747a5c9bf85fed877bae/gsplat/cuda/csrc/ProjectionEWA3DGSFused.cu#L201-L208) · [Opacity accumulation](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/gsplat/blob/937e29912570c372bed6747a5c9bf85fed877bae/gsplat/cuda/csrc/RasterizeToPixels3DGSFwd.cu#L142-L169) · [Expected depth](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/gsplat/blob/937e29912570c372bed6747a5c9bf85fed877bae/gsplat/rendering.py#L760-L768)

Blender stores each mesh-hit Position in world metres. Applying the inverse target pose gives its optical-axis Z. Its camera export converts Blender’s axes to OpenCV using `diag(1,−1,−1,1)`. Its opaque film and illuminated World determine valid reference RGB wherever a ray sees the World. The independently reopened cameras, historical ED probes, PNG/array comparisons and exact file lineage test these interfaces. Historical source-image content and learned scale accuracy are separate empirical questions. The audit invokes no additional trained checkpoint.
